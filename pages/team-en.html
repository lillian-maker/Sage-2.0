SAGE AI TEAM

Specialists work together,
and you decide at the key moments

Each task is led by one specialist who brings in contributors and reviewers as needed; judgments, authorizations and acceptance stay with you and your team. Below is one complete task in the Sage app.

  1. 01

    Kick-off

    The lead aligns with you on a kick-off card: clarifying questions, task brief, acceptance checklist and plan; missing skills or connections wait for your confirmation, and nothing runs until you press Start.

  2. 02

    Multi-specialist orchestration

    The execution graph runs through understand, research, analyze, review and deliver; three specialists research in parallel, and any node that needs authorization stops and waits for you.

  3. 03

    Parallel work

    See what each specialist is doing, which material they are reading and for how long; in the main conversation only the lead reports to you.

  4. 04

    Authorization

    When approving, choose the scope: this time only, this task or this workspace; actions that write to external systems can never be always allowed across a workspace.

  5. 05

    Decide in the Inbox

    Approvals, requests for information, acceptance and invitations all arrive in Inbox · Needs my decision; approve there and the task updates too.

  6. 06

    Acceptance

    Every item on the acceptance checklist links to its evidence in the report or materials; accept when all are met, or send it back with a reason and the lead reworks only that part.

  7. 07

    Versions

    Every change creates a new version you can compare line by line; restoring an old version creates a new one too, so history is never overwritten.

  8. 08

    Reuse

    Save an accepted execution graph as a Playbook to reuse for another market or category, or hand it to an automation to run on a schedule.

COLLABORATION WITH A REASON

Four roles, never an unclear owner

When no specialist fits, Sage Assistant becomes the lead, under the same availability and authorization checks as every specialist.

Lead specialist
Each task has exactly one lead before it starts: it understands the goal, breaks down the steps, organizes collaboration and delivers. Sage recommends one and you can switch at any time.
Contributing specialists
They take clear sub-goals under the lead and work in parallel; the lead cites and combines their results, which are never treated as finished on their own or published directly.
Reviewers
Key conclusions are cross-checked: numbers by Qingzhang, compliance by Anjie, and the whole by Shouheng or Qiuzheng as independent reviewers.
You
You set goals and constraints, confirm the plan, approve controlled actions and accept results. Each approval covers only the targets, actions, scope and time on the confirmation card.

Closed loop

A seven-step loop, with you deciding at the key moments

Every step can be inspected and adjusted.

  1. 01

    Understand

    Build a task ledger: goal, acceptance criteria, known facts, open questions and constraints.

  2. 02

    Assemble and orchestrate

    Pick the lead and contributors on the specialist graph and orchestrate the steps into an execution graph with dependencies.

  3. 03

    Kick-offYou confirm

    Complex tasks start with a kick-off card: clarifying questions, task brief, acceptance checklist and plan.

  4. 04

    Execute in parallel

    Independent steps run at the same time, and each sees only the knowledge it needs and may access.

  5. 05

    Cross-check

    Each step checks itself against its acceptance criteria; key conclusions are cross-checked by a reviewer.

  6. 06

    Repair and replanYou decide

    Only failed steps are redone; after several rounds without progress, Sage pauses and hands the decision to you.

  7. 07

    Deliver and retainYou accept

    Results arrive with sources for your acceptance; proven flows can be saved as Playbooks and reused.

Capability depth

Three graphs ground every orchestration

Each node in the execution graph = one specialist × one skill × the knowledge they are allowed to use

Who

Specialist graph

Common handoffs, upstream and downstream work, and review relationships between specialists. Used to assemble teams: simple tasks stay small, and key conclusions always get a reviewer.

How

Skill graph

Every skill has preconditions and outputs. Steps are orchestrated into an execution graph and checked for connections and authorizations first; a failed skill is replaced by an alternative.

On what basis

Knowledge graph

Business entities such as brands, products, markets, channels, competitors and regulations, with effective dates, sources and access rights, grounding each step and flagging conflicts.

The more you use it, the better it knows your business

Facts that stay
Newly accepted facts flow back to the knowledge graph with their sources, ready for the next task (planned for phase two).
Collaboration that improves
The outcome of every collaboration updates the links between specialists and skills, so teams get assembled more accurately.
Workflows you reuse
A proven execution graph can be saved as a Playbook, reused in one step or run on a schedule by automation.

Find, understand and create specialists

Team collaboration lives in specific places in the app; these are the four you will use most.

Specialist directory
Sage Assistant is pinned on top and 53 digital specialists are grouped by domain; search by name, role or skill and pick one to lead a task.
Specialist profile
See a specialist’s responsibilities, skills, available tools and collaboration network: who they hand off to and who reviews them.
Create a specialist
Create a specialist for your business in five steps: identity and responsibilities, collaboration style, skills, tools and connections, knowledge and context; test before enabling.
Collaboration in a task
A task has a main conversation, side chats and progress; the orchestration view shows what each specialist is doing, kick-off and authorization cards appear in the conversation, and anything that needs you also lands in the Inbox.
